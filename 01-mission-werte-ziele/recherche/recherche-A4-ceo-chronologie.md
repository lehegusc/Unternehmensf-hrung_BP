## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Between 2007 and 2026, BP underwent one of the most turbulent leadership sequences of any major publicly listed company. The oil giant cycled through **five CEOs since 2020 alone** — the count most frequently cited in analyst commentary — driven by crisis, personal misconduct, strategic failure, and investor dissatisfaction. Each leadership transition brought a radically different framing of corporate purpose: from safety-first (Hayward), to steady rebuilding (Dudley), to an explicit "reimagining energy" Net Zero pivot (Looney), to a retreat back toward oil and gas (Auchincloss), and now a new phase of execution-focused stability (O'Neill). The Board itself went through two chairmen removed in scandal (Looney's enabler Helge Lund stepped down; Albert Manifold was fired), compounding the governance story.

## PART 1: THE COMPLETE CEO TIMELINE (2010–2026)

### 1.1 Tony Hayward — *CEO: May 2007 – 1 October 2010*

**Tenure dates:** Hayward was appointed CEO on **1 May 2007**, succeeding Lord John Browne who was forced out by a personal scandal. He stepped down on **1 October 2010**. [Wikipedia: Tony Hayward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Hayward)

**Background:** A petroleum geologist who joined BP in 1982 as a rig geologist, Hayward rose through Exploration & Production, becoming CEO of E&P in January 2003. He was widely seen as Browne's "blue-eyed boy" and the internal favourite to succeed him. [SPE Journal of Petroleum Technology](https://jpt.spe.org/twa/tony-hayward-chief-executive-exploration-and-production-bp)

**Stated purpose and mission:** When Hayward took the CEO role in 2007, he explicitly set BP's priority as safety. He told journalists his number-one task was to focus **"laser-like" on safety and reliability** — a deliberate reaction to Browne's aggressive growth-first culture. [BBC News profile, 25 July 2010](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-10754710); [ABC News, 17 June 2010](https://abcnews.com/Business/bp-ceo-tony-hayward-oil-ninja-congressional-hot/story?id=10931147). He also confronted the aftermath of the 2005 Texas City refinery explosion (15 deaths) and the 2006 Prudhoe Bay pipeline leak which had both occurred under Browne.

**Why he left — Deepwater Horizon:**
- On **20 April 2010**, the Deepwater Horizon drilling rig in the Gulf of Mexico exploded, killing **11 workers** and triggering the largest oil spill in US history. The well gushed for 87 days. [Wikipedia: Deepwater Horizon oil spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_oil_spill)
- Hayward's public handling was catastrophic. He made a series of notorious gaffes, most memorably telling a journalist: **"I'd like my life back"** while victims' families and Gulf communities suffered. [NY Daily News, 19 June 2010](https://www.nydailynews.com/2010/06/19/tony-hayward-bp-ceo-gets-his-life-back-no-longer-in-charge-of-running-gulf-cleanup-operations/)
- On **22 June 2010**, he was stripped of operational control of the spill response and replaced by Bob Dudley. [Christian Science Monitor, 22 June 2010](https://www.csmonitor.com/From-the-news-wires/2010/0622/Tony-Hayward-hands-over-BP-oil-spill-operation-BP-shares-fall)
- BP announced on **27 July 2010** that Hayward would formally step down as CEO on 1 October 2010, replaced by Bob Dudley. BP reported a **$17 billion quarterly loss** the same day. BP's market cap had fallen by roughly **£45 billion ($70 billion)** since the spill. [Bloomberg, 27 July 2010](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2010-07-27/hayward-s-resignation-inevitable-after-bp-spill-undermined-safety-pledge); [PBS NewsHour, 27 July 2010](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/bps-hayward-exits-as-ceo-as-company-reports-17b-loss)
- BP's total Deepwater Horizon liability eventually exceeded **$65 billion**.

**Post-BP:** Hayward became Chairman of Genel Energy (2011) and Chairman of Glencore (2014–2021). He later said BP was "not prepared" for the Gulf crisis. [BBC, 9 November 2010](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-11709027)

**Hayward's personal safety pledge versus the disaster:** The contradiction was profound — the CEO who had come in promising "laser-like safety focus" presided over the worst oil disaster in US history. His predecessor's cost-cutting culture was widely blamed by investigators, though Hayward bore the reputational consequence. [Mother Jones, 28 February 2013](https://www.motherjones.com/environment/2013/02/former-bp-boss-says-deepwater-horizon-wasnt-his-fault/)

### 1.2 Bob Dudley — *CEO: 1 October 2010 – 4 February 2020*

**Tenure dates:** Dudley took over on **1 October 2010** and retired on **4 February 2020**, following the announcement of BP's full-year 2019 results. He had announced his planned retirement in October 2019. His official retirement date was **31 March 2020**. [Reuters: Dudley's decade](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/dudleys-decade-at-the-helm-of-bp-almost-didnt-happen-idUSKBN1WJ1CV/); [BP Board Activity 2019 PDF](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/investors/bp-board-activity-2019.pdf)

**Background:** An American (from Mississippi), Dudley had previously served as President of TNK-BP (BP's Russian joint venture), where he was forced out by Russian partners. He became the **first American to lead BP**. [Washington Post, 28 July 2010](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/national/2010/07/28/bp-offers-new-ceo-scaledback-strategy/728ecce2-4ac8-4bf0-94a8-edef6a4e38cc/); [Al Jazeera, 27 July 2010](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2010/7/27/bp-replaces-ceo-after-spill)

**The rebuilding mission:** Dudley inherited a company in existential crisis. His stated three strategic priorities, set out in February 2011 and repeated throughout his tenure, were:
1. **"To reinforce safety and risk management at the heart of everything we do"**
2. **"To earn back the trust that is so important for a global company"**
3. **"To build long-term value for our shareholders"**

[Bob Dudley speech to World National Oil Companies Congress, 2011 (BP PDF)](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/news-and-insights/speeches/speech-archive/world-national-oil-companies-congress-bob-dudley-2011.pdf)

**"Safer, stronger, simpler":** Through the first half of his tenure, Dudley consistently described his mission as transforming BP into a **"safer, stronger and simpler business."** BP's 2017 Annual Report explicitly used this formula. He sold over $40 billion of assets and reshaped the portfolio around the company's "highest-value opportunities." [BP Annual Report 2017 (ECGI)](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/governance-report-2017.pdf)

**The energy transition framing:** By 2017-2018, Dudley began integrating energy transition language. In his October 2017 speech "Advancing the energy transition," he stated: **"Today we are focused on our dual mission of delivering the energy that the world needs while advancing the low carbon world that we all want."** [BP Dudley speech PDF, Oct 2017](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/news-and-insights/speeches/advancing-the-energy-transition-speech-bob-dudley.pdf). His 2018 strategy framing was around "RIC": **Resilient** hydrocarbons, **Improving** returns, and **Creating** lower carbon businesses. [BP CERAWeek 2018 speech PDF](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/news-and-insights/speeches/cera-week-2018-bob-dudley.pdf)

**2019 AGM (his final):** In his 2019 AGM speech, Dudley stated the strategy was *"resilient for the tough environment we operate in. But flexible and agile, so we can move fast and adapt to change."* [BP AGM CEO Speech 2019 PDF](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/news-and-insights/speeches/bp-annual-general-meeting-ceo-2019.pdf)

**Leadership style:** Dudley was widely described as cautious, methodical, and deliberately low-key — the "quiet man who saved BP." His tenure was stable and produced rising shareholder returns (he increased the quarterly dividend eight times). [AJ Bell / BP PDF profile](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/news-and-insights/bob-dudley-quiet-man-who-saved-bp.pdf)

### 1.3 Bernard Looney — *CEO: 5 February 2020 – 12 September 2023 (resigned)*

**Tenure dates:** Looney succeeded Dudley on **5 February 2020**. He resigned with immediate effect on **12 September 2023** — less than four years into his tenure. His employment was subsequently terminated for serious misconduct in December 2023. [Reuters, 13 September 2023](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-ceo-bernard-looney-resign-ft-2023-09-12/); [AP, 12 September 2023](https://apnews.com/article/bp-ceo-bernard-looney-resigns-0a722eac4c306fa209d9340c62ba52ea)

**Background:** Looney was Irish, born in County Kerry. He had spent his entire career at BP, joining straight from college. He was widely seen as charismatic and media-savvy, and championed a new generation of leadership.

**The Net Zero Pivot — 12 February 2020:**
Just **one week** after becoming CEO, on 12 February 2020, Looney made one of the most dramatic announcements in BP's history. He unveiled the strategy called **"Reimagining Energy, Reinventing BP."**

The centrepiece was a new **corporate purpose**: *"Reimagining energy for people and our planet."* [BP 2020 Annual Report PDF](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/country-sites/nl-nl/netherlands/home/documents/perscentrum/bp-annual-report-and-form-20f-2020.pdf)

BP's net zero announcement stated:
- **Net zero across BP's operations** on an absolute basis by 2050 or sooner
- **Net zero on carbon in BP's oil and gas production** on an absolute basis by 2050 or sooner
- A **50% cut in the carbon intensity** of products BP sells by 2050 or sooner
- Methane measurement at all major oil and gas processing sites by 2023, plus a **50% methane intensity reduction**
- A plan to **cut oil and gas production by 40%** by 2030

[BP Official Press Release, 12 February 2020](https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bernard-looney-announces-new-ambition-for-bp); [CNN, 12 February 2020](https://www.cnn.com/2020/02/12/business/bp-net-zero); [Fortune, 10 August 2020](https://fortune.com/longform/bp-oil-gas-clean-energy-ceo-bernard-looney-petroleum-profits-stock/)

**Key Looney quote:** *"The world's carbon budget is finite and running out fast; we need a rapid transition to net zero."* [BP Fleet Solutions PDF](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/country-sites/en_gb/united-kingdom/home/images/fleet/pdf/bp-fleet-solutions-brochure.pdf)

**Another Looney quote on ambition:** *"We want to be wanted, not just needed."* [Catholic Lisbon Business School, February 2020](https://clsbe.lisboa.ucp.pt/news/bp-announced-new-purpose-and-ambitions-we-want-be-wanted-not-just-needed-edition-23)

**August 2020 strategy update:** BP announced it would **cut oil and gas production by 40%** by 2030 (from 2.5 mboed to 2 mboed) and increase renewables spending from under $1 billion to **$5 billion/year** by 2030, targeting **50 gigawatts of renewable capacity** by 2030 (from 2.5 GW). [SP Global Platts, 19 May 2021](https://www.spglobal.com/platts/en/market-insights/latest-news/natural-gas/051921-bps-looney-says-investors-warming-to-new-strategy-as-green-pledges-grow). BP formally rebranded itself as an **"Integrated Energy Company"** rather than an International Oil Company.

**Why he resigned — Misconduct:**
- In **May 2022**, BP's board received allegations relating to Looney's "conduct in respect of personal relationships with company colleagues." An investigation was conducted with external legal counsel (later identified as Freshfields). At the time, Looney disclosed past relationships and the investigation found no breach of conduct policies. [AP, 12 September 2023](https://apnews.com/article/bp-ceo-bernard-looney-resigns-0a722eac4c306fa209d9340c62ba52ea); [Irish Times, 12 September 2023](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2023/09/12/bps-irish-chief-bernard-looney-resigns-over-past-relationships-with-colleagues/)
- In **September 2023**, further allegations of a similar nature prompted a new review. On **12 September 2023**, Looney informed the board he had **"not been fully transparent"** in his previous disclosures. He resigned with immediate effect. [CNBC, 12 September 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/12/bp-ceo-bernard-looney-will-resign-report-says.html); [New York Times, 12 September 2023](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/09/12/business/energy-environment/bp-bernard-looney-resign.html)
- In **December 2023**, following conclusion of the Freshfields investigation, BP found Looney had **"knowingly misled the board"** by giving "inaccurate and incomplete assurances in July 2022." The board classified this as **"serious misconduct."** BP clawed back approximately **£32 million ($40 million)** in pay/compensation from Looney. [BBC, 13 December 2023](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-67709815); [Reuters, 14 December 2023](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-says-former-ceo-looney-not-get-further-pay-2023-12-13/); [Sky News, 13 December 2023](https://news.sky.com/story/former-bp-boss-forfeits-32m-for-misleading-board-over-workplace-relationships-13029680)

**Legacy tension:** Looney left behind a company described by Le Monde as *"highly profitable but lost in transition."* [Le Monde English, 13 September 2023](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2023/09/13/bp-ceo-resigns-he-leaves-behind-a-company-that-was-highly-profitable-but-lost-in-transition_6134335_19.html)

### 1.4 Murray Auchincloss — *CEO: 12 September 2023 (interim) → 17 January 2024 (permanent) → 18 December 2025 (stepped down)*

**Tenure dates:**
- Named **interim CEO** on 12 September 2023, immediately upon Looney's resignation
- Named **permanent CEO** on **17 January 2024** by the board
- Stepped down on **18 December 2025** — less than two years as permanent CEO

[BP Press Release, 17 January 2024](https://www.bp.com/en_us/united-states/home/news/press-releases/murray-auchincloss-appointed-bp-chief-executive-officer.html); [BP Leadership Transition Press Release, 17 December 2025](https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bp-plc-announces-leadership-transition)

**Background:** A Canadian by birth, Auchincloss joined BP in 1993. He served in various financial roles, including Head of BP's Group Chief Executive's Office (2010-2013). He became CFO in 2020 under Bernard Looney — meaning he was intimately involved in building the green strategy he then reversed. The Times noted he was *"strongly associated with the oil group's disastrous pivot to green energy"* as Looney's right-hand man. [The Times, 18 December 2025](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/murray-auchincloss-why-ousted-from-bp-6njbt92n3)

**The February 2025 Capital Markets Day / Strategy Reset:**
On **26 February 2025**, Auchincloss presented BP's **"Reset BP" strategy** — described as the company's most significant strategic reversal in decades. [BP Press Release: "Growing Shareholder Value: A Reset bp", 26 February 2025](https://www.bp.com/en-US/press-and-publications/press-releases/growing-shareholder-value-a-reset-bp)

Key elements of the reset:
- Oil and gas capex raised **~20% to $10 billion/year**
- Renewable/low-carbon spending cut by **more than $5 billion/year**, to just $1.5–$2 billion/year
- 2030 production target raised to **2.3–2.5 million barrels of oil equivalent per day**
- "$20 billion in asset divestments" through 2027 announced
- Explicitly described as abandoning Looney's 20-fold renewables expansion target
- Strategy framed as: **"grow upstream, focus downstream, invest with discipline in transition"**

[Reuters, 27 February 2025](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/bp-ramps-up-oil-gas-spending-10-billion-ceo-rebuilds-confidence-2025-02-26/); [BBC, 26 February 2025](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c3374ekd11po); [AP, 26 February 2025](https://apnews.com/article/bp-oil-green-reset-1b9cfca4c2da138f83ace86b5945e053); [CNBC, 26 February 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/26/bp-to-ramp-up-fossil-fuel-spending-to-10-billion-in-strategy-reset.html)

**Key Auchincloss quote from the CMD:** *"Today we have fundamentally reset bp's strategy. We are reducing and reallocating capital expenditure to our highest-returning businesses to drive growth, and relentlessly pursuing performance improvements and cost efficiency. This is all in service of growing long-term shareholder value."* [CNBC, 26 February 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/26/bp-to-ramp-up-fossil-fuel-spending-to-10-billion-in-strategy-reset.html)

**Admitting the Looney strategy failed:** At a press briefing, Auchincloss acknowledged BP went **"too far, too fast"** in its efforts to pivot to renewables. [Energy Voice, 26 February 2025](https://www.energyvoice.com/markets/567575/bp-boss-we-went-too-far-too-fast-as-firm-slashes-low-carbon-investment-by-5bn/)

**Did he change the purpose statement?** ⚠️ *Important finding for your research:* Formally, the purpose statement in BP's 2025 Annual Report changed from Looney's *"reimagining energy for people and our planet"* to **"Delivering energy to the world, today and tomorrow."** This new wording appears in both the 2024 and 2025 Annual Reports and on BP's current "Our purpose, beliefs and code of conduct" page. The shift was quiet, not announced with fanfare. [BP Annual Report 2025 PDF](https://www.bp.com/api/files/6cqieuqhq4no/master/33M2iHp8A6d07McKzKqLNP/12b5d4eccb4e02093d1ad9efc0d6a746/bp-annual-report-and-form-20f-2025.pdf); [BP "Who We Are" page, April 2026](https://www.bp.com/about-us/who-we-are)

**Context of his departure — Elliott Management pressure:**
In February 2025, activist hedge fund **Elliott Management** disclosed it had built a stake of **nearly 5%** in BP worth billions, and was pushing for deeper changes. [Reuters, 13 February 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/elliott-discloses-nearly-5-billion-stake-bp-ft-reports-2025-02-13/). Even after the February CMD, Elliott was reportedly not satisfied with the pace and depth of change.

**Albert Manifold's role:**
After the appointment of **Albert Manifold** as BP Chair in **October 2025** (see Part 4), Auchincloss signalled internally that he was open to stepping down if a better leader could be found. Observer (UK) reported *"sharp disagreements over strategy with the oil major's chairman"* preceded the departure. [Observer, 21 December 2025](https://observer.co.uk/news/business/article/bp-installs-new-boss-after-split-over-ditching-green-transition). Manifold reportedly did not believe Auchincloss — as Looney's former CFO and strategic architect of the green pivot — was credible to lead the reversal. The Times noted: *"Murray was seen as having been too much a part of the 'Bernard blueprint' and therefore not credible to pivot back strongly enough."* [Observer, 11 January 2026](https://observer.co.uk/news/national/article/bp-looks-to-new-management-to-re-energise-fortunes)

His departure was framed publicly as voluntary via a "succession planning process," but coverage was universally sceptical: **"BP: beyond petroleum, beyond perdition or just bad performance?"** noted that *"neither Mr Auchincloss nor BP were able to give a credible reason why"* he departed so abruptly. [Baird Maritime, July 2026](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/offshore/column-bp-beyond-petroleum-beyond-perdition-or-just-bad-performance-part-two-of-two-offshore-accounts). Health issues were **NOT** cited in any official documentation — this appears to be an unfounded claim.

**⚠️ Fact-check on health issues:** No credible source confirms health-related reasons for Auchincloss's departure. The public reason is strategic/succession pressure. Do not use health reasons in your paper without a primary source.

### 1.6 Meg O'Neill — *CEO: 1 April 2026 – present*

**Appointment announcement:** 17 December 2025. Start date: **1 April 2026**. [BP Official Press Release, 17 December 2025](https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bp-plc-announces-leadership-transition)

**Historic significance:**
- BP's **first female CEO** — and the **first woman to lead a global oil "supermajor"** [BBC, 18 December 2025](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cq60jq125ljo)
- BP's **first externally-recruited CEO** in its 116-year modern history — breaking the tradition of promoting from within [Corp Digest CEO History](https://corpdigest.com/company/bp/ceo-history)
- Named the **fifth CEO since 2020** by multiple sources [Reuters; Baird Maritime; US News]

**Background — ExxonMobil (1994–2018):**
O'Neill was born in **Boulder, Colorado** (1970; age 55–56). She joined ExxonMobil in **1994** straight from college (B.Sc., M.Sc. engineering credentials). She spent **23–24 years** at ExxonMobil in progressively senior roles including:
- Regional production and development leadership in **Indonesia, Norway, and Canada**
- Vice President, Africa for ExxonMobil Development Company
- Houston-based leadership roles

[Houston Chronicle, 29 July 2022](https://www.houstonchronicle.com/business/energy/article/Woodside-CEO-helped-close-gap-between-Australia-17337099.php); [SIEW 2026 speaker profile](https://www.siew.gov.sg/siew-2026/speakers/2024/meg-o-niell)

**Background — Woodside Energy (2018–2025):**
In **May 2018**, O'Neill was recruited by Woodside Petroleum CEO Peter Coleman (a former ExxonMobil colleague) as **Chief Operating Officer**. On **17 August 2021**, she was appointed **CEO and Managing Director** of Woodside. [Business Council of Australia bio](https://www.bca.com.au/our-people/meg-oneill/)

**What kind of company is Woodside?** Woodside Energy is **Australia's largest oil and gas producer**, headquartered in Perth, Western Australia, and primarily focused on **LNG (liquefied natural gas)**. Under O'Neill, Woodside completed the landmark **acquisition of BHP Petroleum** in 2022, which doubled Woodside's production portfolio and transformed it into the **largest energy company listed on the Australian Securities Exchange**. [Reuters, 18 December 2025](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/sustainable-finance-reporting/incoming-bp-chief-charted-expansive-legacy-australias-woodside-2025-12-18/). Critically, Woodside is a **pure-play fossil fuel producer** with no significant renewables operations — O'Neill's background is almost entirely in hydrocarbons operations and dealmaking.

**Why she was chosen:** BP's board under Albert Manifold was looking for an **operational executor** with a track record in oil and gas, not a strategist associated with BP's green experiment. Reuters noted her reputation for *"operational execution and a renewed focus on oil and gas."* [Reuters, 18 December 2025](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/bp-picks-first-outsider-ceo-meg-oneill-after-abrupt-auchincloss-exit-2025-12-18/)

**Strategic vision — "Consistency":**
O'Neill's first communication to BP staff on **1 April 2026** (her first day) emphasised **consistency and stability** after years of flux. A memo to staff, seen by Reuters, focused on *"accelerating the group's performance"* and *"consistency"* — signalling no immediate new strategic pivot, but a focus on executing the existing reset. [Reuters, 1 April 2026](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/sustainable-finance-reporting/new-bp-ceo-takes-helm-pledging-consistency-staff-note-shows-2026-04-01/)

Her **mandate from the board** was explicitly stated to be: *"strengthen oil and gas output while divesting lower-return clean energy assets."* [Energy Digital, 21 April 2026](https://energydigital.com/news/what-is-bps-new-ceo-meg-oneills-strategy-for-the-firm)

**June 2026 — Organizational restructuring:** On 9 June 2026, O'Neill announced a major structural overhaul effective 1 July 2026: BP would move from a **three-segment** to a **two-segment structure** (Upstream and Downstream), eliminating the middle layer. She described this as making BP *"simpler, stronger and more valuable."* [BP Press Release, 9 June 2026](https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bp-simplifies-organizational-structure-and-announces-leadership-appointments)

**Key O'Neill quote (October 2026):** *"We have not been careful stewards of shareholder capital."* — Said at the Energy Intelligence Forum in London, signalling accountability for past strategic failures. [Ground News/Reuters, 2 November 2026](https://ground.news/article/bp-must-make-better-use-of-shareholder-capital-ceo-says)

**December 2026 Strategy Day:** O'Neill announced she would set out a fresh strategy vision in **December 2026** in New York — her first full investor day as CEO, described as her opportunity to lay out "the next phase" of BP's plans. [Morningstar/Dow Jones, 13 October 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202610131204/bps-new-ceo-to-outline-strategy-as-balance-sheet-strengthens-2nd-update); [RTÉ, 13 October 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1013/1595154-bp-flags-3-4-5-billion-in-impairments-for-third-quarter/)

## PART 2: PURPOSE EVOLUTION — CEO BY CEO

| CEO | Formal Purpose Statement | Core Strategic Framing |
|-----|--------------------------|------------------------|
| **Hayward (2007–2010)** | Not a formal published purpose statement | "Laser-like focus on safety and reliability" |
| **Dudley (2010–2020)** | "Dual mission: delivering the energy the world needs while advancing the low-carbon world" (2017) | "Safer, stronger, simpler"; Safety → Rebuild → Value |
| **Looney (2020–2023)** | *"Reimagining energy for people and our planet"* (Feb 2020) | Net Zero by 2050; Integrated Energy Company; -40% oil & gas output |
| **Auchincloss (2023–2025)** | *"Delivering energy to the world, today and tomorrow"* (quietly changed ~2024) | "Reset BP": grow upstream, focus downstream, discipline in transition |
| **O'Neill (2026–present)** | *"Delivering energy to the world, today and tomorrow"* (inherited) | Consistency, performance, simplification, oil & gas focus |

**Key observation on purpose statement change:** The shift from Looney's *"reimagining energy"* language to the current *"delivering energy to the world, today and tomorrow"* was **gradual and unannounced** — not marked by a dramatic speech or event. ## PART 3: THE "5 CEOs IN 6 YEARS" NARRATIVE — VERIFIED

**Is the count accurate?** Several formulations exist:

| Version | What it counts | Source |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| "Fourth CEO since 2020" | Looney, Auchincloss, (Carol Howle interim?), O'Neill — likely counting Looney, Auchincloss, O'Neill as three permanent CEOs + Howle | C-Store Dive headline, December 2025 |
| "Fifth CEO since 2020" | Looney, Auchincloss (interim), Auchincloss (permanent), Carol Howle (interim), O'Neill | Reuters; Baird Maritime; US News; Energy Voice |
| "Third CEO since 2020" | Looney, Auchincloss (combined interim/permanent), O'Neill | RNZ (Radio New Zealand) |

**Verdict:** The count depends on whether you treat:
1. Auchincloss's interim and permanent phases as one or two roles
2. Carol Howle's three-month interim stint as a CEO tenure

**The most rigorous formulation for your paper:** BP has had **four formal CEOs since 2020** (Looney, Auchincloss, and O'Neill as permanent CEOs, with Howle as interim). However, the **"fifth CEO since 2020"** formulation is the most commonly used in media and reflects the full count including both of Auchincloss's designations and Howle. The CNBC headline *"BP names new CEO — its fourth in 6 years"* (18 December 2025, referring to O'Neill at appointment) is perhaps most defensible. [CNBC, 18 December 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/18/woodside-energys-meg-oneill-to-replace-murray-auchincloss-as-bp-ceo.html/)

The BP board itself compounded instability: both Helge Lund (retired under pressure after shareholder revolt with only 76% support at the 2025 AGM) and Albert Manifold (fired for misconduct after just 7 months) failed in succession planning their own exits gracefully.

## PART 4: BOARD AND CHAIRMAN ROLE

### Carl-Henric Svanberg (Chairman 2010–2018)
BP's chairman during the Dudley era. Svanberg is Swedish and a former Ericsson CEO. He famously referred to Gulf of Mexico victims as *"small people"* during the Deepwater Horizon crisis. He stepped down in 2018.

### Helge Lund (Chairman: 1 January 2019 – 1 October 2025)

**Background:** Norwegian; former CEO of Statoil (now Equinor) and BG Group — both European energy companies with green credentials. He joined BP's board in 2018 and became Chair on 1 January 2019.

**His stated mandate:** Lund said he took over as chairman with two big transitions to handle: (1) replacing a CEO after Bob Dudley's decade (he led the process that resulted in Looney's appointment in October 2019); and (2) navigating BP's shift from a hydrocarbon company to one with interests in clean technologies. [The Times, 16 December 2023](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/bps-chairman-is-sliding-away-on-a-slick-of-poor-decisions-9g32l39f7)

**Did the Board drive the Net Zero pivot?** Evidence strongly suggests **yes**. The 2019 BP Board Activity document states: *"From the beginning of 2019, we made our strategy the central focus of every board meeting."* [BP Board Activity 2019 PDF](https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/investors/bp-board-activity-2019.pdf). The same document says the board decided on the new purpose: *"Our new purpose, to reimagine energy for people and our planet, is supported by a new ambition - for BP to get to net zero by 2050 or sooner."* This language preceded Looney's formal appointment — suggesting the board set the direction, and Looney was chosen in part because he would execute it.

At the February 2025 CMD, Lund personally endorsed the Reset: *"The board believes that this is an important strategic reset for bp and is confident that it, together with rigorous performance management, will deliver improved performance and sustainable value for bp's shareholders."* [BP Reset Press Release, 26 February 2025](https://www.bp.com/en-US/press-and-publications/press-releases/growing-shareholder-value-a-reset-bp)

**Lund's departure:** In **April 2025**, Lund informed the board of his intention to step down, *"likely in 2026."* At BP's April 2025 AGM, he faced a major **shareholder revolt**: nearly **25% voted against his re-election** — an extraordinary figure for an FTSE 100 chairman. [Reuters, 17 April 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-leadership-faces-shareholder-vote-amid-elliott-campaign-climate-ire-2025-04-17/); [Yahoo News UK, April 2025](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/major-shareholder-revolt-against-bp-123212281.html). He officially stood down as chair and board director on **1 October 2025**.

### Albert Manifold (Chairman: 1 October 2025 – 26 May 2026)

**Background:** Irish; former CEO of **CRH plc** (2014–2024), the global building materials company. He had **no previous energy industry experience**, which was notable.

**Appointment:** Announced 21 July 2025; joined board 1 September 2025 as chair-elect; became chair 1 October 2025. [Reuters, 21 July 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/bp-appoints-albert-manifold-chairman-amid-strategy-revamp-2025-07-21/)

**Immediate actions:** Within days of taking the role, Manifold issued an internal memo saying BP may sell assets from its "overly complex" portfolio and pushed for accelerated restructuring. [Energy Voice, 3 October 2025](https://www.energyvoice.com/renewables-energy-transition/581782/bp-chairman-accelerate-oil-gas-refocus/)

**His role in Auchincloss's departure:** The Observer reported Manifold *"apparently didn't rate the former CFO, who fell into the top job when Looney was forced out."* [Observer, December 2025](https://observer.co.uk/news/business/article/bp-installs-new-boss-after-split-over-ditching-green-transition). Yahoo Finance reported: *"Albert Manifold was forthright about his rationale for Auchincloss's removal. He stated that the struggling oil and gas firm needed to become 'simpler, leaner, and more profitable,' emphasizing the necessity for greater 'rigor and diligence' at the executive level."* [Yahoo Finance, 18 December 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bp-disastrous-embrace-net-zero-181700528.html)

**Ousted for misconduct:** On **26 May 2026** — just **seven months** after becoming Chairman — BP's board voted **unanimously** to remove Manifold with immediate effect, citing *"serious concerns related to important governance standards, oversight, and conduct."* Manifold hit back at what he called *"lies."* [Reuters, 27 May 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-removes-chairman-albert-manifold-2026-05-26/); [Telegraph, 26 May 2026](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2026/05/26/bp-is-once-again-gripped-by-embarrassing-boardroom-drama/). He was replaced by **Ian Tyler** (former Balfour Beatty CEO) as interim chairman, then formally appointed.

### Ian Tyler (Chairman: 2026 – present)

Tyler was appointed as **permanent chairman** in late 2026 following a search. [BP Press Releases](https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases). This represents BP's **third Chairman since 2019**.

## PART 5: MURRAY AUCHINCLOSS — DETAILED PROFILE

**His role in the green pivot:** As CFO under Looney, Auchincloss was a key architect of the 2020 strategy. Reuters noted: *"While CFO under Looney, Auchincloss was key in formulating changes to its strategy last February [2023], including slowing down BP's retreat from oil and gas and reducing spending on renewables."* [Reuters, 17 January 2024](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-names-auchincloss-permanent-ceo-2024-01-17/)

## PART 7: CONTRADICTION AND UNCERTAINTIES FLAGS

1. **⚠️ "5 CEOs in 6 years" vs "4 CEOs since 2020":** The exact count varies by source (3, 4, or 5) depending on whether interim designations (Auchincloss September 2023; Howle December 2025) are counted separately. CNBC used "fourth in 6 years" in December 2025; by May 2026, Telegraph, Reuters, and others were using "fifth since 2020." The discrepancy is real, not an error.

2. **⚠️ Auchincloss departure reason:** BP's official wording was "succession planning process." No official reason beyond strategic/succession tension has been provided. Health rumours appear to circulate but have **no sourced confirmation**. The Observer, The Times, Yahoo Finance, and others all pointed to the Manifold-Auchincloss strategic disagreement as the real driver.

3. **⚠️ When did the purpose statement actually change?** The "reimagining energy" language was phased out between 2022 and 2025 without a formal announcement. Some regional BP web pages still show legacy "reimagining energy" language (e.g., bp.com/en_au/australia) even as the corporate level carried the new "delivering energy" wording. This is worth noting as a sign of internal communication lag.

4. **⚠️ Albert Manifold's misconduct:** The full details of why Manifold was removed have **not been made public** by BP beyond *"serious concerns about governance standards, oversight, and conduct."* Manifold contested the characterisation publicly. [Telegraph, 26 May 2026]

5. **⚠️ BP's current Net Zero ambition:** Despite the strategic reset, BP has **not formally abandoned** its net zero ambition. The 2025 Annual Report and sustainability pages still state: *"Our ambition is to be a net zero company by 2050 or sooner, and to help the world get to net zero."* However, BP retired its **Scope 3 net zero production target** in the 2025 reset and backed away from specific renewable capacity targets. [BP People and Planet Aims, February 2025](https://www.bp.com/api/files/6cqieuqhq4no/master/1mONt6arxEWFez3nYtupga/c68e1763ba224c58817b226d86c8e086/bp-people-and-planet-aims-at-a-glance.pdf); [Reclaim Finance assessment, April 2025](https://reclaimfinance.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BP-Assessment-2025.pdf)
