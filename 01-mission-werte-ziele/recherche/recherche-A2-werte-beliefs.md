## 1. CURRENT OFFICIAL VALUES: What BP Currently States

### 1.1 The Current Framework: "Who We Are"

BP's current official corporate values are not labeled as "values" in the traditional sense. Instead, since around **2021–2022**, BP rebranded its cultural foundation as a **"culture frame"** called **"Who we are"**, consisting of **three core beliefs** (not five values). This was a deliberate terminological shift — BP explicitly "chose to drop the 'values & behaviours' language and adopt 'Beliefs'" to create clear distance from the old approach. [The Team employee-engagement case study, https://theteam.co.uk/work/bp-beliefs/]

**The three beliefs, with exact wording from bp.com (as of April 2026):**

> *"'Who we are' defines what we stand for at bp. It builds on our best qualities and those things that are most important to us – our commitment to safety, to speaking up if something doesn't seem right, compliance, care for others and working together as one integrated bp team – while putting an even greater focus on how we can take bp to the next level."*
> [https://www.bp.com/about-us/who-we-are/our-purpose-beliefs-and-code]

**Belief 1 — Live Our Purpose:**
- *Safety comes first* — Look after each other and our company – in the real world and online
- *Make a positive impact* — Everywhere we work – and with everyone we work with
- *Do the right thing* — Always follow our code

**Belief 2 — Play To Win:**
- Know the competition
- Keep improving
- Be accountable

**Belief 3 — Care For Others:**
- Be kind
- Prioritize the team
- Put yourself in other people's shoes

[Source: BP.com "Our purpose, beliefs and code of conduct", https://www.bp.com/about-us/who-we-are/our-purpose-beliefs-and-code, retrieved April 7, 2026; BP Careers "Who we are", https://careers.bp.com/about]

### 1.3 How Many Values / What Are Their Names?

Formally, BP now has **three beliefs** (not values, in its own language):
1. **Live our purpose**
2. **Play to win**
3. **Care for others**

Each belief has **three sub-elements**, making nine sub-elements in total. BP also describes the framework as being "underpinned by our commitment to safety," which functions as a meta-value beneath and across all three beliefs. [BP Sustainability Report 2025, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-sustainability-report-2025.pdf]

## 2. VALUES EVOLUTION: How BP's Values Have Changed

### 2.1 The Original Five Values (Pre-2021)

For many years before the Looney era, BP operated with **five explicit "values"** that were widely published in annual reports, the code of conduct, and marketing materials:

1. **Safety** — "Safety is our core value... underpinned by our OMS, which sets out how we aim to deliver safe, reliable, compliant and sustainable operations."
2. **Respect** — "We respect the world in which we operate. It begins with compliance with laws and regulations... We depend on the relationships we have and respect each other and those we work with."
3. **Excellence** — Pursuing excellence in operations and delivery.
4. **Courage** — Having the courage to do the right thing.
5. **One Team** — Believing success comes from working together.

**Exact formulation from 2018 Annual Report:**
> *"Pursuing this approach, BP is guided by its values of safety, respect, excellence, courage and one team."*
[BP Annual Report 2018, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-annual-report-and-form-20f-2018.pdf]

The 2020 Annual Report still used the same five-values language: *"Our values of safety, respect, excellence, courage and one team represent the qualities and actions we wish to see in bp."* [BP Annual Report 2020, https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/country-sites/nl-nl/netherlands/home/documents/perscentrum/bp-annual-report-and-form-20f-2020.pdf]

These five values were visible throughout the Bob Dudley era (2010–2020) and were explicitly linked to the Code of Conduct in that period.

### 2.2 The Transition: 2020–2022

The transition happened in stages:

- **February 12, 2020:** New CEO Bernard Looney took office and launched a new **purpose** ("reimagine energy for people and our planet") and a net-zero ambition. However, the **five values** (Safety, Respect, Excellence, Courage, One Team) were *retained* in the 2020 Annual Report alongside the new purpose. [BP Annual Report 2020]
- **August 2020:** BP's "Reinventing bp" investor day (Kerry Dryburgh, EVP People & Culture) described plans for an agility-focused culture reset. [bp Capital Markets Week 2020, https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/investors/bpweek/bpweek-reinventing-bp.pdf]
- **2021:** BP's Annual Report for 2021 introduced the concept of the new **"Who we are"** culture frame with three beliefs alongside an updated culture agenda. The 2022 Annual Report refers to the board being "consulted in the development of bp's new culture frame and specifically our new core beliefs, which we refer to as 'Who we are'." [Scribd 2022 Annual Report excerpt, https://id.scribd.com/document/664768558/GB0007980591-2022]
- **2022:** BP formally commissioned external communications firm "The Team" to **position the new beliefs in the minds of employees**. A case study states: *"In 2022, bp asked The Team to help position a new set of beliefs in the minds of employees."* [The Team, https://theteam.co.uk/work/bp-beliefs/]
- **Post-2022:** The five-values language ("safety, respect, excellence, courage, one team") largely disappears from BP's main corporate communications, replaced entirely by the three-belief "Who we are" framework.

**Key transition summary:**

| Period | Framework | CEO |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-2020 | Five values: Safety, Respect, Excellence, Courage, One Team | Bob Dudley |
| 2020 | Both: New purpose + five values still in annual report | Bernard Looney (from Feb 5, 2020) |
| 2021–2022 | Transition: "Who we are" framework introduced | Bernard Looney |
| 2022 onwards | Three beliefs: Live our purpose, Play to win, Care for others | Looney → Auchincloss (from Sep 2023) → Murray Auchincloss (permanent from Jan 2024) |

### 2.3 Did Values Change When the Purpose Changed (February 2025)?

**Short answer: The beliefs themselves did NOT change in February 2025, but the purpose statement did.**

On **February 26, 2025**, BP announced its "Reset BP" strategy at a Capital Markets Update. The purpose was changed from:
> *"Reimagining energy for people and our planet"* (adopted February 2020)

to:
> *"Deliver energy to the world, today and tomorrow."*

[BP Net Zero Aims at a Glance, updated 26 February 2025, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-net-zero-aims-at-a-glance.pdf; BP Annual Report 2025 Governance Report, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-governance-report-2025.pdf]

The **three beliefs** (Live our purpose / Play to win / Care for others) remained in place unchanged after the February 2025 strategy reset. The sustainability aims were narrowed, but the culture frame language was retained.

**What changed in February 2025:**
- Purpose statement (new wording)
- Sustainability framework (narrowed to: net zero operations, net zero sales, people, biodiversity, water)
- Strategy (back to oil and gas growth, ~$10bn/yr in fossil fuels, transition spending cut from ~$7bn to ~$1.5–2bn/yr)
- Investment allocation (fossil fuels dramatically increased)

**What did NOT change:**
- The three beliefs of "Who we are"
- The Code of Conduct structure
- The safety-first language

### 2.4 Under CEO Meg O'Neill (Since April 2026)

Meg O'Neill became BP's first female CEO and first "outside" CEO (having come from Woodside Energy, an Australian company) effective **April 1, 2026**. [Reuters, https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/sustainable-finance-reporting/new-bp-ceo-takes-helm-pledging-consistency-staff-note-shows-2026-04-01/; BP press release, https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bp-plc-announces-leadership-transition]

Her initial communication to employees (April 1, 2026 staff note) pledged **"consistency"** while accelerating performance. She explicitly did not announce a new values framework. [Reuters, https://boereport.com/2026/04/01/new-bp-ceo-takes-helm-pledging-consistency-staff-note-shows/]

Under O'Neill, the major cultural shift has been toward a **stronger performance culture** with clearer accountability — not a values rebranding. Specific actions:
- **June 2026:** Announced restructure into two business segments (Upstream, Downstream), replacing the previous three-segment structure [BP press release, https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bp-simplifies-organizational-structure-and-announces-leadership-appointments]
- **June 30, 2026:** Appointed **Sonya Adams as EVP People & Culture**, described as "passionate about the role of culture, leadership, and capability in enabling strategic shifts." [BP press release, https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bp-announces-leadership-appointments]
- **July 2026 LinkedIn post:** O'Neill stated: *"We need to make better, faster decisions, reduce complexity and sharpen accountability"* and *"I want the organization focused on execution"* [Emery Little analysis, https://emerylittle.com/meg-oneill-first-100-days-at-bp/]
- Energy Intelligence described O'Neill's strategic focus as: *"better defining BP's corporate culture, executing a consistent strategic focus, managing investor expectations and improving the balance sheet."* [Energy Intelligence, https://shop.energyintel.com/products/bp-strategic-outlook]

As of November 2026, O'Neill had not announced a new values/beliefs framework. The three beliefs ("Who we are") remained the official culture frame, though significantly sharpened through performance-accountability language.

## 3. BP CODE OF CONDUCT: Key Principles and Connection to Values

### 3.1 Current Code Structure

BP's Code of Conduct was **updated in 2024** — explicitly described in BP America Limited's 2024 Annual Report as: *"bp's code of conduct, which was updated in 2024, and 'Who we are' beliefs set clear expectations for how bp, the company and the board operate to maintain a reputation for high standards of business conduct."* [BP America Limited 2024, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-america-limited-2024.pdf; London Stock Exchange announcement, https://www.rns-pdf.londonstockexchange.com/rns/3874T_1-2025-7-31.pdf]

**From BP's own website, the code is described as:**
> *"bp's code of conduct is our guide to doing the right thing. It's at the foundation of 'Who we are' and everything we do at bp – from the boardroom to the front line – and highlights our absolute commitment to safety, ethics, compliance and speaking up."*
[https://www.bp.com/about-us/who-we-are/our-purpose-beliefs-and-code]

**Structural relationship:**
> *"Our code sets standards and expectations for how we do the right thing and empowers our employees to speak up without fear of retaliation. It is the foundation of 'Who we are', our culture frame and puts safety first. Together with our Safety Leadership Principles and OMS, our code helps us make safe and ethical decisions, act responsibly, comply with applicable laws."*
[BP Annual Report 2024, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-annual-report-and-form-20f-2024.pdf]

### 3.2 Key Principles in the Code

The Code is organized around five broad thematic sections (as documented in Australia's version, representative of the global code structure [https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/country-sites/en_au/australia/home/products-services/fuels/code-of-conduct.pdf]):

1. **Operating (Safety & Sustainability)** — Protecting health, safety, security and environment; safe, responsible, reliable operations
2. **Our People** — Treating people fairly with dignity; equal opportunity; harassment-free workplace; protecting privacy
3. **Business Partners and Suppliers** — Expectations for ethical supply chain conduct
4. **Governments and Communities** — Compliance with laws; anti-corruption; community engagement
5. **Assets and Financial Integrity** — Anti-bribery; financial reporting; conflicts of interest

### 3.3 Anti-Bribery / Anti-Corruption

The 2024 Annual Report explicitly states that the code of conduct **prohibits bribery or corruption in any form**. [BP Annual Report 2024]

### 3.4 Application and Compliance

- Applies to **all bp employees, officers, and board members**
- Employees must confirm annually that they have read, understood, and complied with it
- Regular mandatory training and communications support implementation
- 2024 update specifically strengthened expectations on **workplace relationships and conflicts of interest** — directly triggered by the Looney resignation scandal [Business Insider, https://www.businessinsider.com/bp-new-office-relationships-policy-bernard-looney-disclose-or-fired-2024-6; RTE, https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2024/0610/1453936-bp-tightens-workplace-relationships-policy/]

### 3.5 Connection Between Code, Beliefs, and Purpose

BP draws an explicit three-way connection:

**Purpose → Beliefs → Code:**
- The **purpose** ("deliver energy to the world, today and tomorrow") sets the direction
- The **beliefs** (Live our purpose / Play to win / Care for others) define "how we are" as a culture
- The **code** specifies behavioral expectations and mandatory rules derived from those beliefs

The BP 2025 Strategic Report states: *"Our code is the foundation of 'Who we are' and everything we do at bp...Together with our Safety Leadership Principles and OMS, it helps us act responsibly, comply with applicable laws, and implement our sustainability frame."* [BP 2025 Strategic Report, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-ar2025-strategic-report.pdf]

## 4. VALUES IN PRACTICE: Operationalization

### 4.1 Safety Architecture: Values → Principles → Systems

BP has built a layered operational architecture for translating its core safety value into practice:

**Layer 1 — Culture Frame:** "Who we are" (three beliefs) — sets mindset
**Layer 2 — Safety Leadership Principles (SLPs):** Five specific principles for behavior:
1. We genuinely care about each other
2. We will not compromise our focus on safety
3. We encourage and recognize speak up
4. We understand how work actually happens
5. We learn from mistakes and events

[BP Safety Performance, https://www.bp.com/about-us/sustainability/safety-performance; BP Safety Leadership Principles slides, https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/country-sites/en_us/united-states/home/documents/products-and-services/contractor-safety/safety-leadership-principles-slides.pdf]

**Layer 3 — Operating Management System (OMS):** A single group-wide framework for managing operational activities; brings together requirements on health, safety, security, environment, reliability, and compliance. "It defines a consistent approach to managing our operational activities – helping us to improve performance in delivering safe, reliable and compliant operations." [BP Sustainability approach page, https://www.bp.com/en/global/corporate/sustainability/our-approach-to-sustainability.html]

**Layer 4 — Life-Saving Rules and Process Safety Fundamentals:** Industry-standard operational rules adopted from IOGP, applied at the frontline. [BP Process Safety Fundamentals, https://www.bp.com/about-us/safety/process-safety-fundamentals]

### 4.2 Training Programs

- **Mandatory annual code compliance confirmation** — all 90,000+ employees
- **Well-control training program** — mandatory for deepwater staff following Deepwater Horizon, accredited by IADC and IWCF [BP Bly Report Update 2012, https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/business-sites/en/global/corporate/pdfs/sustainability/archive/bly-report-and-archive/bp-bly-sri-update-october-2012-final.pdf]
- **CHAOS™ simulation program** — "Combined Human And Operational Skills" immersive exercises for offshore crews; integrates theoretical instruction with hands-on simulation [CHAOS™ case study, https://assets.eu.ctfassets.net/0v2vk30knkad/.../2522-CHAOS-BP-case.pdf]
- **grow@bp platform** — comprehensive online platform for all employees, 24/7 access, covering skills from reskilling to leadership [BP careers, https://careers.bp.com/job-description/RQ111145]
- **BP Learning Centers** — in Houston and the UK, with high-definition distance learning capability [Mechdyne case study, https://www.mechdyne.com/av-vr-solutions/case_study/bp-learning-centers/]
- **Safety Leadership Principles rollout** — actively rolled out across BP in 2023–2024 with effectiveness measurement [BP Safety at bp America, https://www.bp.com/en_us/united-states/home/who-we-are/safety.html]

### 4.3 Incidents Where Values Were Tested

**Texas City Refinery Explosion (2005):**
The US Chemical Safety Board (CSB) found deep contradictions between BP's stated safety values and actual organizational behavior:
- *"A 'check the box' mentality was prevalent at Texas City, where personnel completed paperwork and checked off on safety policy and procedural requirements even when those requirements had not been met."* [CSB Final Investigation Report BP Texas City, https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/20/csbfinalreportbp.pdf]
- The CSB found that "while most attention was focused on the injury rate, the overall safety culture and process safety management program had serious deficiencies." [CSB investigation, https://www.csb.gov/at-the-time-of-2010-gulf-blowout...]
- Personnel were not encouraged to report safety problems; some feared retaliation — directly contradicting the stated values of safety and courage.

**Deepwater Horizon (2010):**
- Investigations found that BP's executive messaging had not communicated a consistent and meaningful message about process safety. [MIT Sloan, https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2020-03/BP%20and%20the%20Deepwater%20Horizon%20Disaster%20of%202010.IC_.pdf]
- The Baker Panel found "an absence of reporting of abnormal situations for fear of blame and reprisals" — contradicting the courage and one team values then in force. [CSB/DNFSB presentation, https://www.dnfsb.gov/sites/default/files/.../phpr_2014827_25001.pdf]
- The accident highlighted that BP had not effectively learned lessons from Texas City in translating values into process safety practice. [CSB Macondo Vol. 3, https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/20/macondo_vol3_final_20160527.pdf]

**Bernard Looney Resignation (2023):**
- CEO Bernard Looney resigned on September 12, 2023 after failing to fully disclose personal relationships with colleagues to the board — a direct violation of ethical standards and the code of conduct's conflict-of-interest provisions.
- This triggered the 2024 update to the Code of Conduct specifically strengthening disclosure requirements for workplace relationships. [Reuters, https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-begins-search-new-ceo-with-no-clear-front-runner-2023-09-13/; Personnel Today, https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/bp-colleague-relationships-staff-must-disclose/]

**Albert Manifold Board Removal (2026):**
- BP unanimously removed Chair Albert Manifold in May 2026 after just eight months in post, citing "serious concerns related to important governance standards, oversight and conduct." [Reuters, https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-removes-chairman-albert-manifold-2026-05-26/]
- Multiple sources alleged "bullying" and "overbearing" behavior — directly contradicting the stated "Care for others" belief (be kind, prioritize the team). [BBC, https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cjrppppwvwlo]
- Manifold denied the allegations and disputed the characterization of his conduct. [UPI, https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/05/27/ousted-chair-albert-manifold-bp-board/4521779891120]
- This was the third major governance/conduct crisis at BP in less than five years (after Looney's resignation and the Helge Lund chairmanship controversy).

The CSB investigations into both Texas City (2005) and Deepwater Horizon (2010) demonstrated a persistent gap between the stated "Safety" value and the actual operational culture. BP's own materials acknowledge this: the Safety Leadership Principles (introduced in 2022–2023) were explicitly designed to address cultural shortcomings in safety behavior. [BP Safety article, https://www.bp.com/en_us/united-states/home/news/features-and-highlights/here-is-how-bp-thinks-about-safety.html]

**Tension 3: "Make a positive impact" vs. climate strategy rollback**

(See Section 6 for detailed analysis.)

Under CEO Auchincloss and continuing under O'Neill, BP has undergone significant headcount reductions as part of its "simpler, leaner" restructuring. This sits in some tension with the "Care for others" belief.

## 6. BP'S NET ZERO AMBITION VS. VALUES: The Climate Contradiction

### 6.1 The Original 2020 Commitment

On **February 12, 2020**, CEO Bernard Looney announced:
> *"We are heading to net zero. There is no turning back."*

This included:
- Net zero ambition by 2050 or sooner
- Cut oil and gas production by **40%** by 2030 (vs. 2019)
- Scale up annual investment in renewables tenfold to $5bn by 2030
- Build 50GW of low-carbon generation capacity

[BP press release, https://www.bp.com/press-and-publications/press-releases/bernard-looney-announces-new-ambition-for-bp]

### 6.2 The Gradual Retreat: 2023–2025

- **February 2023:** Oil production cut target reduced from **40%** to **20–30%** by 2030
- **October 2024:** Reuters revealed BP had **abandoned the oil output reduction target** entirely. *"BP (BP.L) has abandoned a target to cut oil and gas output by 2030."* [Reuters, https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-drops-oil-output-target-strategy-reset-sources-say-2024-10-07/]
- **February 26, 2025:** "Reset BP" — official strategy announcement:
  - Transition spending cut from ~$7bn to **$1.5–2bn/yr**
  - Oil and gas investment increased **~20% to $10bn/yr**
  - Production target changed from a *reduction* to an *increase* to **2.3–2.5 million boe/day by 2030**
  - CEO Auchincloss said BP had gone **"too far, too fast"** and that faith in green energy was **"misplaced"**
  - Purpose changed from "reimagining energy for people and our planet" to "deliver energy to the world, today and tomorrow"
  [Standard, https://www.standard.co.uk/business/business-news/bp-slashes-netzero-spending-by-nearly-ps4bn-in-renewed-oil-and-gas-focus-b1213368.html; BBC, https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c3374ekd11po; Reuters commentary, https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/bps-green-failure-offers-big-oil-lesson-moderation-2025-02-27/]

### 6.3 What Remained After the 2025 Reset

BP did NOT abandon net zero entirely. What it retained:
- **Net zero ambition by 2050** on Scope 1 and 2 (operational) emissions
- **Net zero sales** ambition by 2050
- **Operational emissions target:** 45–50% reduction by 2030 (slightly diluted from 50%)
- Notably: BP *exceeded* its 2025 operational target, achieving **37%** reduction against a targeted **20%** from 2019 baseline [BP Sustainability Report 2025, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-sustainability-report-2025.pdf]

What BP **retired**:
- Scope 3 net zero production target
- Oil and gas output reduction target
- Large-scale renewable energy growth ambition

[BP Net Zero Aims at a Glance, updated 26 Feb 2025, https://www.bp.com/api/files/...bp-net-zero-aims-at-a-glance.pdf; Reclaim Finance assessment, https://reclaimfinance.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BP-Assessment-2025.pdf]

### 6.4 The Specific Values-Strategy Contradiction

**The core tension:** BP's "Who we are" beliefs include "Make a positive impact — Everywhere we work and with everyone we work with" and the company's purpose says it will "deliver energy to the world, today and tomorrow." BP continues to describe itself as committed to net zero by 2050.

Yet:
- **Capital allocation:** Between 2025–2027, BP plans **$10.5bn/yr** in oil and gas vs. only **$800m** in low carbon energy — a ~13:1 ratio favoring fossil fuels [Reclaim Finance, https://reclaimfinance.org/site/en/assessment-of-oil-and-gas-companies-climate-strategy/]
- **Production trajectory:** BP plans to *increase* production to 2.3–2.5 mmboe/day by 2030, which Reclaim Finance calculates would leave BP **57% above** the IEA Net Zero Emissions pathway [Reclaim Finance BP Assessment 2025, https://reclaimfinance.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BP-Assessment-2025.pdf]
- **Shareholder response:** Legal & General Investment Management voted against BP chair re-election at the 2025 AGM, citing climate backtracking. Almost **25% of shareholders** rejected the new chair [Business and Human Rights Centre, https://www.business-humanrights.org/ar/.../legal-general-challenges-bp-leadership-over-abandoned-green-targets/]

**External criticism:**
- **Global Witness** labeled BP's reset a *"masterclass in greenwashing"* [Global Witness billboards, https://globalwitness.org/en/press-releases/global-witness-billboards-highlight-bps-climate-backtracking-to-investors/]
- **Bloomberg** noted BP had "gone through a series of broken climate promises" [Bloomberg, https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2025-corporate-climate-broken-promises/]
- **Reclaim Finance:** *"BP has simply abandoned its scope 3 target for 2030 and its commitment to achieve carbon neutrality across its entire operations on an absolute basis by 2050 or sooner."* [Reclaim Finance European companies report, https://reclaimfinance.org/site/en/2025/05/19/the-backtracking-of-major-european-oil-and-gas-companies-on-climate/]
- **ClientEarth** had previously filed the world's first lawsuit against BP advertising for greenwashing (2021 complaint). [ClientEarth, https://www.clientearth.org/projects/the-greenwashing-files/]

**BP's defense:**
BP argues its values actually support the reset: the "Play to win" and "Be accountable" beliefs justify a strategy that creates real shareholder value rather than chasing unrealizable climate targets. The purpose change to "deliver energy to the world, today and tomorrow" also reframes what "positive impact" means — energy security, affordability, and reliability become central values alongside environmental considerations.

The new purpose explicitly removes the green framing of the old "reimagining energy for people and our planet," substituting pragmatic energy delivery language. Auchincloss said in August 2025: *"I believe we should own the fact we went too far, too fast in the transition… We need to be a company that delivers strong financial performance for our shareholders."* [Fortune, https://fortune.com/article/bp-ceo-murray-auchincloss-gas-clean-energy-big-oil-bernard-looney/]

**Under CEO O'Neill (2026):**
O'Neill has further reinforced the fossil-fuel pivot. In June 2026, BP eliminated the "Gas and Low Carbon Energy" business segment entirely, folding renewable assets into "other businesses." [Fortune, https://fortune.com/2026/06/09/bp-ceo-meg-oneill-shakeup-strategy/; Yahoo Finance, https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/energy/articles/bp-ceo-meg-o-neill-185201432.html] O'Neill has stated the strategy is "upstream, downstream, and trading" — explicitly anchored in oil and gas. [Q3 2026 earnings call, https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/bp-bp-q3-2026-earnings-174315572.html]
